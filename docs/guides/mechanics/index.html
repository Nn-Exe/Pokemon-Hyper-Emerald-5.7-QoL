Getting started

Game Mechanics

Difficulty modes, level caps, the 24 starters, Mega Evolution, Z-Moves, Dynamax and the other rules Hyper Emerald changes under the hood.

The hack changes a lot under the hood. These are the rules you need to know before you pick a starter, and the systems you will keep running into.

Decide your difficulty before you leave the bedroom

The difficulty is chosen at the wall clock in your bedroom at the start of the game and cannot be changed afterwards.

Difficulty modes

Four modes exist. The fourth is hidden: interact with the notebook next to the PC in your bedroom and pick the red “Not Ready” option ten times, then use the clock.

Standard (标准)Tied to badges (about Lv 18 before Gym 1, Lv 23 after)Allowed in the field; formal battles ban themGyms and leagues: no duplicate species or held items, no box legendaries or mythicals.

Level caps and obedience

  • In every mode except Easy, Pokémon stop gaining experience at the current cap. The cap rises with each badge and league win.
  • Pokémon above the cap disobey, and you cannot catch Pokémon that are too strong for your current badge count. An NPC in Sinnoh puts it plainly: “Since I’ve only got one Gym Badge, it sometimes ignores my orders.”
  • Gym and league battles follow tournament rules. Status moves are unreliable there: “In battles against Gym Leaders and the Hoenn League’s Elite Four, moves that inflict status conditions and the like have no effect.”
  • Shops unlock stock by badge count (Revives, for example).
  • Consumable items used in trainer battles are given back afterwards.

Starters and early gifts

  • On the starter screen press ▶ to page through all 24 starters from Kanto to Galar. Hisuian lines appear as Cyndaquil, Oshawott and Rowlet; picking one of those gets you a second starter from Professor Birch after you beat your rival.
  • The Hoenn trio can be caught later in a pen at the lab once you have the Running Shoes, with three perfect IVs.
  • Your PC starts with a Shiny Charm, an EV-IV Display and Power items.
  • Petalburg Woods: Professor Oak offers a Let's Go Pikachu or Let's Go Eevee (they do not evolve; a tutor in Fortree teaches their partner moves).
  • Route 104: an NPC gives a Wimpod. Rustboro, house top-right of the gym: trade for a Zorua; the same NPC swaps Zorua between its Unovan and Hisuian forms.

More gifts, trades and hidden one-off battles are listed in Side Content & Gift Pokémon.

Battle systems added by the hack

Mega Evolution

Needs the Mega Bracelet: after beating Norman, talk to your mother in Littleroot. Her line: “This is a gift that the president of Devon asked your father to pass along… Your father thought it was still too early for you to receive such a precious band, so he left it with me!” Mega Stones are hidden items and rewards; see Mega Stones, Z-Crystals & Key Items.

Z-Moves

Needs the Z-Ring, won from the trainer behind the Fortree City Pokémon Center. Z-Crystals come from the 18 Totem Pokémon hidden around Hoenn; collecting all 18 is also a story gate in the post-game.

Dynamax and Gigantamax

Opens in the post-game on World Championship Island, where Leon (or Sonia, if you missed him) hands you the Dynamax Band. Dynamax is only possible at Power Spots (the Championship battles, Leon’s rematches, some story fights). Gigantamax forms come from Max Mushrooms and a Max Soup NPC on the island, who treats the first Pokémon in your party.

Other combat changes

  • Moves, abilities and items through Generation 8; turn order is recalculated mid-turn as in Sword and Shield.
  • Trainer AI is stronger across the board; the higher modes let it read your move.
  • Formal battles (gyms, leagues, the World Championships) use tournament rules: exactly six Pokémon brought, pick three or four, doubles for the Sinnoh League and Championships.

Training and quality of life

WhatHow
Exp. ShareBuilt in: full experience to battlers, half to the bench. “It works just by sitting in your Bag.” With the patch, using it from the Bag switches it off and on.
EVs / IVsThe whole team gets EVs. An EV checker and an IV judge stand at the Solaceon Day Care; the starting PC has the EV-IV Display. Hyper Training on Champion Island shows 31 for a trained stat.
TMs and HMsReusable. With the HM in the Bag and its badge, field moves work even if nobody can learn them (Flash from the party menu in a dark cave). Every TM can be bought on World Championship Island after the Sinnoh League.
Nature changeTrainer’s School in Rustboro: the Unova Gym Leader hands out Mints. The hack hides them behind a true/false quiz; the patch opens the Mint offer straight away, and the summary shows the new nature.
Ability changeAbility Pill (regular abilities) and Ability Patch (hidden ability) drop as held items from Zigzagoon on Route 101. Professor Sycamore also sells them for Zygarde Cells.
Trade evolutionsTalk to the boy in the house east of Fallarbor and answer “No” (counts as a trade); an NPC in the Fortree Pokémon Center does the same. Hold the required item first.
Move relearnerThe patch adds a Moves option in the party menu (no Heart Scale). The hack’s own tutors want Heart Scales; farm them from Luvdisc with the Old Rod.
Egg movesInherited as in Sword and Shield, from either parent. With the Oval Charm (Day-Care Man on Route 117, after the Hoenn League) Eggs appear about twice as often.
BikesMach and Acro Bikes from the house right of the Mauville Pokémon Center. With the patch you hold both at once. The Sky Pillar needs the Mach Bike.
RepelsThe hack asks “use another?” when a repel ends; the patch adds L to start one.
Headbutt treesRam big trees with a Pokémon for tree encounters (“Pokémon live up in the trees. A Headbutt can get their attention.”).
Evolution stonesThief from wild Pokémon on Mt. Pyre’s Route 122 side (Vulpix: Fire Stone, Murkrow: Dusk Stone, Litwick: Dawn Stone, all 1%). An Eevee fan in Sinnoh trades stones for coloured shards.
RematchesPost-game rematches against gym leaders and story characters happen on the second floor of your house in Littleroot.

Getting around

  • Hoenn: as vanilla, plus a ride device and the News Tracker that reports where roaming Pokémon are. Steven’s Island, Champion Island, Strange Island and Southern Island each get a red square on the Fly map once you can fly there.
  • Sinnoh: every town has Ride Pokémon that fly you between towns you have visited. The summoning device is in the Team Galactic warehouse in Veilstone. The patch’s Sinnoh Map does the same from a map: move the box, press A. Bibarel climbs rock walls with the ride gear; Route 206 requires a rented bike from the Eterna bike shop (pass the assistant’s exam first).
  • Hisui: Rei is the fast-travel NPC between the safe zones, and his Rotom PC gives you box access. The Sinnoh Map shows Hisui there and flies you with Braviary.

Things closed or cosmetic

  • The Global Terminal, Trade Hall, Veilstone Game Corner and Seven Stars Restaurant in Sinnoh are decorative.
  • The World Championship tournament itself is a placeholder in v5.7 (“The World Championships will officially open in the future”); the island hosts cameo battles and Dynamax Power Spots.
  • Sprites occasionally glitch after warps. An NPC explains it: “If this happens, just switch screens and it will go back to normal.”
