The hack changes a lot under the hood. These are the rules you need to know before you pick a starter, and the
systems you will keep running into.

<Callout type="warn" title="Decide your difficulty before you leave the bedroom">
  The difficulty is chosen at the **wall clock in your bedroom** at the start of the game and cannot be changed
  afterwards.
</Callout>

## Difficulty modes

Four modes exist. The fourth is hidden: interact with the **notebook next to the PC in your bedroom** and pick the
red "Not Ready" option ten times, then use the clock.

| Mode | Level cap | Items in battle | Other rules |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Easy** (养生) | None | Allowed | Plays like an official game. Legendaries allowed everywhere. |
| **Standard** (标准) | Tied to badges (about Lv 18 before Gym 1, Lv 23 after) | Allowed in the field; formal battles ban them | Gyms and leagues: no duplicate species or held items, no box legendaries or mythicals. |
| **Challenge** (挑战) | As Standard | Banned in trainer battles | The AI sometimes predicts your move. |
| **Lunatic** (疯子, hidden) | As Standard | Banned | Every trainer has perfect IVs and full EVs; the AI predicts most moves. |

## Level caps and obedience

- In every mode except Easy, Pokémon **stop gaining experience at the current cap**. The cap rises with each badge
  and league win.
- Pokémon above the cap **disobey**, and you cannot catch Pokémon that are too strong for your current badge count.
  An NPC in Sinnoh puts it plainly: "Since I've only got one Gym Badge, it sometimes ignores my orders."
- Gym and league battles follow tournament rules. Status moves are unreliable there: "In battles against Gym
  Leaders and the Hoenn League's Elite Four, moves that inflict status conditions and the like have no effect."
- Shops unlock stock by badge count (Revives, for example).
- Consumable items used in trainer battles are given back afterwards.

## Starters and early gifts

- On the starter screen press <Key>▶</Key> to page through **all 24 starters** from Kanto to Galar. Hisuian lines
  appear as <Mon name="Cyndaquil" />, <Mon name="Oshawott" /> and <Mon name="Rowlet" />; picking one of those gets
  you a second starter from Professor Birch after you beat your rival.
- The Hoenn trio can be caught later in a pen at the lab once you have the Running Shoes, with three perfect IVs.
- Your PC starts with a <Item name="Shiny Charm" />, an <Item name="EV-IV Display" /> and Power items.
- Petalburg Woods: Professor Oak offers a <Mon name="Let's Go Pikachu" /> or <Mon name="Let's Go Eevee" /> (they
  do not evolve; a tutor in Fortree teaches their partner moves).
- Route 104: an NPC gives a <Mon name="Wimpod" />. Rustboro, house top-right of the gym: trade for a
  <Mon name="Zorua" />; the same NPC swaps Zorua between its Unovan and Hisuian forms.

More gifts, trades and hidden one-off battles are listed in [Side Content & Gift Pokémon](/guides/side-content/).

## Battle systems added by the hack

### Mega Evolution

Needs the <Item name="Mega Bracelet" />: after beating Norman, talk to your mother in Littleroot. Her line: "This
is a gift that the president of Devon asked your father to pass along… Your father thought it was still too early
for you to receive such a precious band, so he left it with me!" Mega Stones are hidden items and rewards; see
[Mega Stones, Z-Crystals & Key Items](/guides/mega-stones-key-items/#mega-stones).

### Z-Moves

Needs the <Item name="Z-Power Ring" label="Z-Ring" />, won from the trainer behind the Fortree City Pokémon
Center. Z-Crystals come from the 18 Totem Pokémon hidden around Hoenn; collecting all 18 is also a story gate in
the post-game.

### Dynamax and Gigantamax

Opens in the post-game on World Championship Island, where Leon (or Sonia, if you missed him) hands you the
<Item name="Dynamax Band" />. Dynamax is only possible at Power Spots (the Championship battles, Leon's rematches,
some story fights). Gigantamax forms come from <Item name="Max Mushroom" label="Max Mushrooms" /> and a Max Soup
NPC on the island, who treats the first Pokémon in your party.

### Other combat changes

- Moves, abilities and items through Generation 8; turn order is recalculated mid-turn as in Sword and Shield.
- Trainer AI is stronger across the board; the higher modes let it read your move.
- Formal battles (gyms, leagues, the World Championships) use tournament rules: exactly six Pokémon brought, pick
  three or four, doubles for the Sinnoh League and Championships.

## Training and quality of life

| What | How |
| --- | --- |
| Exp. Share | Built in: full experience to battlers, half to the bench. "It works just by sitting in your Bag." With the patch, using it from the Bag switches it off and on. |
| EVs / IVs | The whole team gets EVs. An EV checker and an IV judge stand at the Solaceon Day Care; the starting PC has the EV-IV Display. Hyper Training on Champion Island shows 31 for a trained stat. |
| TMs and HMs | Reusable. With the HM in the Bag and its badge, field moves work even if nobody can learn them (Flash from the party menu in a dark cave). Every TM can be bought on World Championship Island after the Sinnoh League. |
| Nature change | Trainer's School in Rustboro: the Unova Gym Leader hands out Mints. The hack hides them behind a true/false quiz; the patch opens the Mint offer straight away, and the summary shows the new nature. |
| Ability change | <Item name="Ability Pill" /> (regular abilities) and <Item name="Ability Patch" /> (hidden ability) drop as held items from <Mon name="Zigzagoon" /> on Route 101. Professor Sycamore also sells them for Zygarde Cells. |
| Trade evolutions | Talk to the boy in the house east of Fallarbor and answer "No" (counts as a trade); an NPC in the Fortree Pokémon Center does the same. Hold the required item first. |
| Move relearner | The patch adds a *Moves* option in the party menu (no Heart Scale). The hack's own tutors want Heart Scales; farm them from <Mon name="Luvdisc" /> with the Old Rod. |
| Egg moves | Inherited as in Sword and Shield, from either parent. With the <Item name="Oval Charm" /> (Day-Care Man on Route 117, after the Hoenn League) Eggs appear about twice as often. |
| Bikes | Mach and Acro Bikes from the house right of the Mauville Pokémon Center. With the patch you hold both at once. The Sky Pillar needs the Mach Bike. |
| Repels | The hack asks "use another?" when a repel ends; the patch adds <Key>L</Key> to start one. |
| Headbutt trees | Ram big trees with a Pokémon for tree encounters ("Pokémon live up in the trees. A Headbutt can get their attention."). |
| Evolution stones | Thief from wild Pokémon on Mt. Pyre's Route 122 side (<Mon name="Vulpix" />: Fire Stone, <Mon name="Murkrow" />: Dusk Stone, <Mon name="Litwick" />: Dawn Stone, all 1%). An Eevee fan in Sinnoh trades stones for coloured shards. |
| Rematches | Post-game rematches against gym leaders and story characters happen on the second floor of your house in Littleroot. |

## Getting around

- **Hoenn**: as vanilla, plus a ride device and the <Item name="News Tracker" /> that reports where roaming Pokémon
  are. Steven's Island, Champion Island, Strange Island and Southern Island each get a red square on the Fly map
  once you can fly there.
- **Sinnoh**: every town has **Ride Pokémon** that fly you between towns you have visited. The summoning device is
  in the Team Galactic warehouse in Veilstone. The patch's <Item name="Sinnoh Map" /> does the same from a map:
  move the box, press <Key>A</Key>. <Mon name="Bibarel" /> climbs rock walls with the ride gear; Route 206 requires
  a rented bike from the Eterna bike shop (pass the assistant's exam first).
- **Hisui**: Rei is the fast-travel NPC between the safe zones, and his Rotom PC gives you box access. The Sinnoh
  Map shows Hisui there and flies you with Braviary.

## Things closed or cosmetic

- The Global Terminal, Trade Hall, Veilstone Game Corner and Seven Stars Restaurant in Sinnoh are decorative.
- The World Championship tournament itself is a placeholder in v5.7 ("The World Championships will officially open
  in the future"); the island hosts cameo battles and Dynamax Power Spots.
- Sprites occasionally glitch after warps. An NPC explains it: "If this happens, just switch screens and it will go
  back to normal."
